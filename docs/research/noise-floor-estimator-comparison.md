# Noise-floor estimator comparison for podcast automixing

## Scope

The automixer does not need a denoised signal. It needs a conservative, explainable estimate of each isolated microphone's local background level so that the existing ownership fallback can ask whether a plausible microphone is materially above its background. The inputs are synchronized, which makes `no microphone has speech` a stronger noise-candidate condition than per-mic VAD alone.

## Recommendation

Start with a **VAD-conditioned local quantile**, with digital-silence rejection, speech-padding/guard intervals, temporal smoothing, and an explicit confidence/fallback policy. It is the best first implementation because it reuses the existing VAD, directly estimates the low-energy baseline needed by the ownership rule, behaves deterministically, and remains easy to inspect in diagnostics.

Do not choose the quantile, window, guard interval, smoothing, or SNR margin by intuition alone. Benchmark them, alongside the alternatives below, on labeled podcast stems. Create an evaluation/design ticket now; defer the production estimator choice until that ticket records corpus results. Minimum-statistics and IMCRA should be advanced baselines, not the first production implementation.

## Comparison

| Method | Strengths here | Important weaknesses here | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **VAD-conditioned local quantile** | Simple; deterministic; robust to high-energy speech contamination; local windows track changing HVAC/fan/room tone; synchronized stems let global VAD reject bleed that a single mic's VAD misses. A quantile estimates exactly the low-energy baseline used by the fallback. | Depends on VAD recall and enough genuinely quiet material; digital silence/mutes bias it downward; window and quantile are tunable assumptions; sorting/history has some memory and compute cost. Cohen notes that temporal-quantile noise estimators require sorting and retained spectral history, although the cost is modest for one broadband value. | **Best first candidate.** Add global no-speech gating, guard padding, silence rejection, confidence, smoothing, and fallback to a longer window/global estimate. |
| **Two-component Gaussian mixture over log energy** | Produces soft membership probabilities and can separate a low-energy population from foreground energy even when the amount of quiet audio varies; log energy is a more plausible modeling space than linear power. Mature EM implementations expose convergence, regularization, repeated initialization, and BIC. | Assumes two Gaussian-shaped populations; real recordings may contain digital silence, room tone, bleed, speech, and intermittent noises as three or more overlapping/skewed modes. Component identities can become unstable in short windows; EM is initialization-sensitive and may fail to converge; selecting the lower-mean component does not prove it is noise. | **Useful benchmark, not default.** Consider only after silence rejection and minimum sample/confidence checks; compare two vs. three components by held-out decision accuracy, not likelihood alone. |
| **KDE and the low-energy mode** | Nonparametric: can reveal skewed or multimodal low-level distributions without asserting Gaussian components; visually excellent for diagnostics and understanding a corpus. | Bandwidth critically controls the result: too small creates spurious modes, too large merges noise and speech. The lowest mode may be digital silence or a rare mute state rather than room tone. Mode tracking can jump between peaks across local windows and needs extra rules for prominence, support, and continuity. | **Best as an analysis/visualization tool.** Too many secondary choices for the first production estimator unless evaluation shows a stable low-energy mode across the corpus. |
| **Robust clustering of non-speech frames** (for example DBSCAN/HDBSCAN over log energy plus optional spectral features) | Can include features beyond broadband level and can label sparse events as outliers. Density-based methods do not require every observation to belong to a cluster; HDBSCAN integrates DBSCAN over varying distance thresholds and selects stable clusters. | In one-dimensional log energy this is more machinery than a quantile. Results depend on distance scaling, density/minimum-cluster parameters, and a policy for selecting the "noise" cluster. Noise that drifts continuously may not form a compact cluster, and added spectral features make behavior harder to explain and validate. | **Only compelling with richer features.** If broadband energy is all that is available, prefer the local quantile. If used, choose the persistent low-energy cluster with temporal continuity and report confidence. |
| **Median + MAD over VAD-rejected frames** | Extremely cheap and deterministic. The median and MAD resist tail outliers; NIST describes MAD as less affected by extreme observations than standard deviation. A threshold such as `median + k × MAD` also estimates normal background variation, not merely location. | The median is the center of candidate frames, not a lower floor. It works only if VAD rejection leaves a majority of representative background. MAD assumes a useful central/symmetric population, has relatively low efficiency for normal data, and can collapse to zero with quantized/digital-silent frames. Choosing `k` merely replaces the quantile choice with another calibration choice. | **Strong fallback/baseline.** Good for confidence and outlier rejection around VAD-filtered noise; less direct than a quantile for defining the low-energy baseline. NIST's `S_n` is a robust alternative if asymmetry becomes a demonstrated problem. |
| **Minimum statistics (spectral)** | Does not require perfect hard VAD; tracks minima of smoothed power per frequency band and includes statistical bias compensation. Frequency resolution can distinguish steady hum/rumble from speech-band foreground energy. | A raw minimum is biased low, so correct smoothing and bias compensation are essential. Search-window length controls adaptation; spectral state and aggregation back to one ownership score add complexity. It solves a harder spectral-noise problem than the present broadband fallback requires. | **Advanced baseline.** Evaluate if failures are caused by changing or spectrally concentrated noise. Do not reduce it to an uncorrected rolling minimum. |
| **IMCRA (spectral)** | Designed for nonstationary noise, weak speech, and low SNR. It recursively averages spectral power with a time- and frequency-dependent smoother controlled by signal-presence probability; two smoothing/minimum-tracking passes reduce speech contamination. The paper reports better estimation error than its comparison method under adverse conditions. | Highest implementation and validation burden here; additional latency/state/parameters; produces a noise PSD that must be converted into the automixer's K-weighted broadband decision statistic. Its benefits in speech enhancement do not automatically imply fewer ownership mistakes. | **Most capable advanced baseline, not the first implementation.** Adopt only if a podcast corpus shows material decision gains over the conditioned local quantile. |

## Suggested evaluation ticket

1. Assemble synchronized stems containing clean pauses, continuous speech, crosstalk, laughter/breaths, inserted digital silence, HVAC/fan changes, hum, handling noise, and two-speaker overlap.
2. Label whether each mic is a legitimate foreground owner per 20 ms frame (or in coarser reviewed regions).
3. Compare the current global 20th percentile against: conditioned local quantile, median/MAD, two-component GMM, KDE mode, and one spectral advanced baseline.
4. Tune only on a development subset; report false attenuation of legitimate audio as the primary risk, then missed bleed attenuation, stability/jitter, runtime, and explainability.
5. Require confidence/fallback behavior for windows with too little global non-speech audio.
6. Keep the estimator interface capable of returning `floor_db[track, frame]` plus confidence, so the ownership threshold remains separate and independently testable.

## Sources

- Silero VAD's official repository documents speech timestamps, supported sample rates, thresholds, silence duration, and speech padding: [Silero VAD](https://github.com/snakers4/silero-vad) and [`utils_vad.py`](https://github.com/snakers4/silero-vad/blob/master/src/silero_vad/utils_vad.py).
- Scikit-learn's official [`GaussianMixture`](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.mixture.GaussianMixture.html) documentation describes EM fitting, multiple initializations, convergence reporting, covariance regularization, and BIC.
- Sheather and Jones identify bandwidth selection as a critical step in KDE: [A Reliable Data-Based Bandwidth Selection Method for Kernel Density Estimation](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/53/3/683/7028194). Scikit-learn documents bandwidth as the KDE smoothing parameter in [`KernelDensity`](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.neighbors.KernelDensity.html).
- Scikit-learn's official clustering documentation describes [DBSCAN](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/clustering.html#dbscan) as finding dense regions and marking noise, and [HDBSCAN](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.cluster.HDBSCAN.html) as integrating DBSCAN over varying epsilon values to find stable clusters.
- NIST defines [median absolute deviation](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/software/dataplot/refman2/auxillar/mad.htm), explains its resistance to extremes in [Measures of Scale](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda356.htm), and documents MAD's symmetry/efficiency limitations and the robust [`S_n` alternative](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/software/dataplot/refman2/auxillar/sn_scale.htm).
- Rainer Martin, [Noise Power Spectral Density Estimation Based on Optimal Smoothing and Minimum Statistics](https://www.iks.rwth-aachen.de/publikationen/publications-detail//martin01c/), develops an unbiased minimum-statistics noise estimator from optimally smoothed PSD estimates and the statistics of spectral minima. His later paper explains why minimum estimates require [bias compensation](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sigpro.2005.07.037).
- Israel Cohen, [Noise Spectrum Estimation in Adverse Environments: Improved Minima Controlled Recursive Averaging](https://israelcohen.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/SAP_Sep2003.pdf), describes IMCRA's frequency-dependent recursive averaging, signal-presence probability, two-pass smoothing/minimum tracking, and adverse-condition evaluation. The paper also discusses limitations of hard-VAD, minimum-statistics, and temporal-quantile estimators.
